Why the bots don't trade every minute

Short version: trading faster doesn't earn faster — it pays the same fee more often, on smaller moves.

To test it properly I ran the same strategy on DOGE over one identical 90-day window at five speeds, with position size held constant so only the candle length changed — and ran every one of them twice, once with real fees and once with fees set to zero:

CandleMove available (ATR)Cost as share of thatPer trade, freePer trade, real fees
1 minute0.031%8.15×−0.019%−0.319%
5 minutes0.125%2.01×−0.049%−0.349%
15 minutes0.289%0.87×+0.143%−0.158%
1 hour0.719%0.35×too few trades in 90 days to judge
4 hours1.600%0.16×too few trades in 90 days to judge

Two things fall out of this. First, the fee costs the same 0.30% per trade at every speed — it does not shrink just because you're trading faster. Second, the amount price actually moves does shrink: a 1-minute candle offers 0.031% of movement versus 1.6% on a 4-hour candle, about 50× less. Same toll, much smaller journey.

The 15-minute row is the clearest proof. Trading it for free would genuinely make money — +0.143% per trade. Charge the real 0.30% and the exact same trades lose −0.158%. The strategy was right and the fees still ate it.

At 1 and 5 minutes it's worse than that: even with zero fees there's no edge to begin with (−0.019%, −0.049%). So speeding up doesn't just cost more, there's nothing there to collect.

Your own parked doge_scalp_1m bot is the real-world version: on 38 trades it had never seen, it won 15.8% and lost 0.29% per trade. That's the biggest test sample of any bot here — so that's not bad luck, it's the arithmetic above showing up in practice.

The honest way to get more trades is more coins on the slower candles, not faster candles on one coin. That's what the bot list below does.

"They're green — why aren't they buying?"

Fair question, and it got tested rather than argued about. Three entry rules, same exits, same fees, every bot, on data none of them had seen:

RuleTradesAvg returnPer tradeBots that made money
Only on the crossing candle (what runs now)146+1.88%+1.29%15 of 16
Buy whenever it's above (green = in)951−3.51%−0.34%3 of 16
Buy whenever price is over its line1082−5.13%−0.36%5 of 16

Buying whenever it's green takes 6.5× more trades and turns a profit into a loss. It's the same arithmetic as above — 805 extra trades × 0.30% a trade is most of the damage, and the rest is entering after the move already happened. The strict rule is annoying to watch, and it's earning that patience.

The uncomfortable number from that same test: simply buying the coins and holding them returned +10.97% on average over that stretch, which beat all three rules. Worth knowing.

Outside evidence agrees: a study of 19,646 Brazilian day traders found 97% of those who kept at it for 300+ days lost money, and Barber & Odean's study of 66,465 households found the highest-turnover traders earned 11.4%/yr versus 18.5% for the lowest — a 7-point penalty for simply trading more. Honest caveat on the table above: 90 days isn't enough history for the 1h and 4h rows to produce a meaningful number of trades, so those two are left blank rather than filled with noise.
